## 📍 Site-Specific Observation Logs

### Case Study 1: Gomti River Lowland Corridors (Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh)
* **Date & Context:** July–August 2023 & 2024 monsoon surge periods.
* **Observed Locations:** Gomti Barrage road link, Shaheed Path elevated feeder underpasses, and Hazratganj drainage confluence.
* **On-Ground Reality:**
  * When the Gomti River crest exceeds 109.50 m MSL, low-level service roads and underpasses accumulate between 45 cm and 75 cm of floodwater.
  * Direct transit from Indiranagar/Gomti Nagar to King George’s Medical University (KGMU) Trauma Centre and Dr. Ram Manohar Lohia Hospital becomes impassable for standard ambulance vehicles.
  * Drivers relying on Google Maps were observed entering the flooded Gomti Barrage route because the map indicated "Clear Road (Green)" due to absent vehicular traffic.

---

### Case Study 2: Yamuna Floodplains & Ring Road Lowlands (Delhi NCR)
* **Date & Context:** July 2023 Yamuna River peak discharge (208.66 m MSL record crest).
* **Observed Locations:** Kashmere Gate ISBT, Monastery Market underpass, Pragati Maidan tunnel, and ITO intersection.
* **On-Ground Reality:**
  * The Ring Road connecting North and Central Delhi was submerged under 60 cm to 1.4 m of muddy river backflow.
  * Emergency vehicles transferring critical ICU patients between North-East Delhi (GTB Hospital) and Central Delhi (LNJP Hospital / GB Pant) were stranded or forced onto 90-minute detours through congested arterial roads.
  * Lack of dynamic depth awareness caused three separate emergency vehicle breakdowns due to water intake in air filters.

---

### Case Study 3: Ganga–Yamuna Confluence Drainage Belt (Prayagraj, Uttar Pradesh)
* **Date & Context:** Annual monsoon drainage backflow alerts (Daraganj, Salori, and Bagambari lowlands).
* **Observed Locations:** Bakshi Bandh lowlands and GT Road underpasses.
* **On-Ground Reality:**
  * Backflow through urban storm drains inundates arterial access routes under 50 cm+ water before river flood warnings are officially broadcast.
  * Patients in need of urgent dialysis and cardiac care could not access Swaroop Rani Nehru (SRN) Hospital without high-clearance military truck intervention.

---

### Case Study 4: Himalayan Foothills & Torrential Riverbeds (Dehradun, Uttarakhand)
* **Date & Context:** Cloudburst events in the Rispana and Bindal river valleys.
* **Observed Locations:** Sahastradhara Road crossings, Rispana bridge feeders, and Raipur arterial road.
* **On-Ground Reality:**
  * Sudden cloudbursts generate high-velocity gravel debris and localized water flows reaching 40–60 cm depth within 20 minutes.
  * Debris boulders physically block narrow mountain roads, requiring instant reroute calculations to Government Doon Medical College Hospital.

## 🎙️ Field Interview Summaries & Beneficiary Quotes

### Interview 1: Emergency Ambulance Driver (Dial 108 Fleet, Lucknow)
> *"When the river swells or heavy rain falls, our biggest fear is entering an underpass. From 30 meters away, a 60 cm pool of water looks like a 5 cm puddle. Once the front tires go in, water hits the air intake, and the engine cuts out immediately. If there is a cardiac patient in the back, we are completely helpless. Standard GPS apps have no idea the water is there."*
> 
> **Key Insight:** Drivers require proactive water depth alerts with clear detour routes before approaching hazardous low-elevation segments.

---

### Interview 2: Emergency Department In-Charge (Trauma Triage Medical Officer)
> *"During mass casualty events or severe storms, multiple ambulances rush to the main hospital simultaneously without any prior coordination. Within an hour, our ICU beds and ventilators are 100% full. We have no choice but to divert ambulances to other hospitals 15 kilometers away, losing vital time for trauma resuscitation."*
> 
> **Key Insight:** Ambulance dispatch must be coupled with real-time bed capacity reservation at the receiving emergency department.

---

### Interview 3: State Disaster Management Operations Officer
> *"We receive river level forecasts from the Central Water Commission, but this information stays on command room screens as regional charts. There is no automated pipeline that converts a 1-meter river rise into turn-by-turn navigation alerts for ambulances and rescue teams on the ground."*
> 
> **Key Insight:** Bridging macro hydrological APIs with micro turn-by-turn routing algorithms is an urgent national priority.
